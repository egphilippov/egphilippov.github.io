## The most interesting hypothesis for Tamarisk

I think an even deeper question may be more productive:

> **Can it be proven that any sufficiently advanced form of life in an infinite-time universe must learn to extract free energy from processes that current physics does not yet fully account for?**

In other words, rather than rejecting the second law of thermodynamics, investigate:

* new physical degrees of freedom;
* new cosmological cycles;
* unknown sources of negentropy;
* processes in which local complexity can persist for arbitrarily long periods.

This is a research program, not an assertion.

---

I would even create a separate Tamarisk branch:

> **Infinite Viability Research**

with the central question:

> **“Under what minimal physical assumptions is open-ended evolution possible over an infinite span of time?”**

This formulation is close to contemporary astrophysics, complexity science, and artificial life. It does not require rejecting existing theories; instead, it organizes known mechanisms that might sustain complexity over long periods.

## Personal approach notes

**Tamarisk Project** 🌱

Do not try to read 300 pages at a time.

A better approach is to accumulate knowledge steadily:

* find an interesting paper → save the PDF;
* write 2–3 sentences explaining why it matters;
* assign tags;
* that is all.

Even **one high-quality paper per day** adds up to about 365 papers a year. This pace is more sustainable than a week-long marathon followed by burnout.

* 🛠️ 20% — current projects (ProgressAction, etc.)
* 📚 10% — fundamental Tamarisk research.

This balance helps keep Tamarisk in view without letting it disappear from your attention.

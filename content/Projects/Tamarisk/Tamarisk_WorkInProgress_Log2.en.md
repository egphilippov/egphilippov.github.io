# Log2

This is a sensible idea. Not just a collection of links, but a **knowledge tree** that gradually becomes an “operating system for survival.” 🌱

I would build a **knowledge base**, not a blog.

For example:

```text
Infinite Viability
│
├── Foundations
│   ├── What Is Infinite Viability?
│   ├── Antifragility
│   ├── Evolution
│   ├── Autopoiesis
│   └── Complex Adaptive Systems
│
├── Biology
│   ├── Aging
│   ├── Repair Mechanisms
│   ├── Longevity
│   ├── Microbiome
│   └── Regeneration
│
├── Civilization
│   ├── Existential Risks
│   ├── Governance
│   ├── Economics
│   └── AI Alignment
│
├── Mathematics
│   ├── Information Theory
│   ├── Control Theory
│   ├── Networks
│   ├── Category Theory
│   └── Game Theory
│
├── Engineering
│   ├── Energy
│   ├── Distributed Systems
│   ├── Robotics
│   └── Biotechnology
│
└── Reading List
```

For each article:

* a brief description (5–10 lines);
* why it matters for infinite survival;
* links to related articles;
* a confidence level (established / hypothesis / speculation).

This is far more valuable than an ordinary list of links.

## Authors

To populate it, I would start with authors who repeatedly appear in the literature on long-term sustainability:

* John von Neumann
* Norbert Wiener
* Claude Shannon
* W. Ross Ashby
* John Holland
* Stuart Kauffman
* Ilya Prigogine
* Elinor Ostrom
* Nassim Taleb
* Karl Friston
* David Deutsch
* Nick Bostrom (as a source for framing risks, even if you disagree with his conclusions)
* Aubrey de Grey
* Michael Levin
* Melanie Mitchell

## Other stuff

It is also useful to create a separate **“Papers Worth Rereading”** section—not hundreds of links, but 30–100 works that are genuinely worth revisiting. Over time, such a catalog becomes much more valuable than an endless collection of bookmarks.
